Frequently Asked Questions About European Time Zones

What are the main time zones in Europe?

Europe primarily spans four standard time zones: Western European Time (WET, UTC+0), Central European Time (CET, UTC+1), Eastern European Time (EET, UTC+2), and Further-eastern European Time (FET, UTC+3).

Which time zone do France and Germany use?

Major European countries like France, Germany, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Poland, and Sweden follow Central European Time (CET). During Daylight Saving Time (DST), they transition to Central European Summer Time (CEST, UTC+2) for optimal daylight hours.

What time zone does the United Kingdom follow?

The United Kingdom and Ireland follow Greenwich Mean Time (GMT / WET, UTC+0) during the winter months and switch to British Summer Time (BST, UTC+1) during the summer period.

When does Europe change its clocks for Daylight Saving Time?

All modern European countries observe Daylight Saving Time concurrently, moving clocks forward on the last Sunday of March and transitioning back one hour on the last Sunday of October. Eastern nations like Romania, Finland, and Greece follow EET (UTC+2) and adjust automatically.
