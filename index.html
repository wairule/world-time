What are the main time zones in Europe?

Europe spans four primary standard time zones: Western European Time (WET/GMT, UTC+0), Central European Time (CET, UTC+1), Eastern European Time (EET, UTC+2), and Moscow Time (MSK, UTC+3). Use this time tool to view Europe time for each zone.

When does Europe switch to Daylight Saving Time?

Most European countries switch to Daylight Saving Time (Summer Time) on the last Sunday of March and return to Standard Time on the last Sunday of October each year, which shifts Europe time offsets.

Which countries use Central European Time (CET)?

Central European Time (CET, UTC+1) covers France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Poland, Austria, Sweden, Hungary, Czechia, Belgium, Switzerland, and several other countries. During summer, it becomes CEST (UTC+2) and updates time accordingly.

Which European countries share the same time zone as the UK?

The UK, Ireland, and Portugal all use Western European Time (WET, UTC+0) in winter. Iceland also stays on UTC+0 year-round but does not observe daylight saving. During summer, the UK and Ireland switch to BST (UTC+1) while Portugal switches to WEST (UTC+1).

What is the time difference between Europe and the US?

Depending on the zone, Europe is 5 to 7 hours ahead of New York and 8 to 10 hours ahead of Los Angeles. The gap narrows by 1 hour during the weeks when Europe has already switched to Daylight Saving Time but the US has not (or vice versa) — usually mid-March and late October.

Do all European countries observe Daylight Saving Time?

No. Iceland, Russia, Belarus, and Turkey keep the same clock all year. The rest of Europe switches on the last Sunday of March and back on the last Sunday of October. Even within the EU, the policy is under review — the European Parliament voted in 2019 to end seasonal clock changes, but implementation has been postponed indefinitely.
